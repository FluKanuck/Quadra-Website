Ground penetrating radar

Used largely to locate non-metallic pipes, ground penetrating radar (GPR) is an essential tool in the locator's toolbox. The common “lawnmower” style of GPR unit is used for utility locating in subsurface soils (125 MHz to 370 MHz). A higher frequency GPR unit is required to locate rebar, conduits and post-tension cables embedded in concrete.

More about GPR

Different soil types, soil geochemistry and object depths all produce different exploration depths for a GPR unit. Sands, gravels and bedrock give a much deeper range than seawater, concrete (a highly conductive material) or clays.

GPR transmits high-frequency radio signals into the ground. Reflected signals return to the receiver and are shown on the unit's screen. The unit measures the time a pulse takes to travel to and from the target, which indicates its depth and location.

Concrete scanning GPR

To locate objects such as rebar, conduits and post-tension cables embedded in concrete, a high frequency GPR system is used (1,000 MHz). The data is collected in grid pattern line scans to map slab anomalies and create a 3D picture of the slab.

Quadra operates a fleet of concrete scanning equipment for residential developments, commercial developments, bridge work and sub-slab assessment projects.

More about Ferroscan

The newest tool in the concrete scanning toolbox is a Ferroscan system, suited to scanning large areas of concrete quickly.

Ferroscan provides accurate depth of cover measurements for reinforcement (rebar) at depths of up to 4 in (100 mm) and displays a clear 2D image of the reinforcement on the monitor for on-the-spot structural analysis and depth of cover assessment.

It records scan data automatically over lengths of up to 30 metres and up to 9 Imagescans. Uses include rebar verification and analysis, checking concrete cover over large areas for structural repair work, building acceptance inspections and quality control, and avoiding rebar hits when coring and hammer drilling. Structural assessment reports can include statistics and 2D/3D views of areas of up to 45 x 45 m.

Electromagnetic (EM) locating

EM utility locators are commonly used for tracing metallic pipes and utility cables. They consist of a separate transmitter and receiver. The transmitter emits a radio frequency EM field that induces secondary fields in nearby metallic pipes and cables. The receiver detects these fields and accurately locates and traces the pipes or conduits.

Another approach is to sweep areas for the 60 Hz signal emanating from electrical lines.
